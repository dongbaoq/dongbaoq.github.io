While reading the paper "E-statistics, Group Invariance And Anytime-valid testing" by Muriel Felipe Perez-Ortiz, Tyron Lardy, Rianne de Heide and Peter D. Grunwald, "amenablity" of Lie group are concerned as important feature.

The famous Haar measure exists for locally compact groups. While this measure guarantees the basic measure theory of Lie groups, it can break on sophisticated settings.

Amenability is equivalent to the existence of a linear functional $m:L^{\infty}(G)\rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ that satisfies

(1) $m(1)=1$

(2) $m(f)\ge 0$ for $f\ge 0$

(3) $m(x.f)=m(f)$ for all $x\in G$

Amenable Lie group has good averaging functional. Beside the good measure on Lie group, the extremely complex structure of Lie group may not assign good "average".

Such a good structure (relatively small) are fulfilled when $G$ is solvable or compact. The equivalence condition of amenability: Folner condition also illustrates this.

Folner condition: There exists a sequence of increasing compact set $F_n$ that for any compact $K$,
$$\lim_{n\rightarrow \infty} \frac{\mu(F_nK)}{\mu(F_n)}=1$$

Since the compact set are good (first, it has finite measure in measure-theoretic perspective and secondly in topological perspective, is much similar to finite sets), these sequence guarantees the entire $G$ could be approximated by these good sets, hence not complex.

The examples of non-amenable Lie group are first, free group generated by two elements (the most intuitive example for non-amenable Lie group), $GL(n,\mathbb{R})$ for $n\ge2$, and $SL(2,\mathbb{R})$ which does not contain free group generated by two elements.
